# Regional Location Index & Strategic Atlas: Petkotkweyak & Siknikt
**District:** Siknikt (*Drainage Area / Chignecto Isthmus*)  
**Traditional Territory:** Mi'kma'ki  
**Scope:** Urban Ruins, Tidal Estuaries, Choke Points, and the Trail Network  

---

## 1. Geographic Overview & The Reclaimed Map

The colonial map labeled this region "Greater Moncton, Westmorland, and Albert County." In the post-collapse era, that arbitrary grid is replaced by the true physical terrain: a powerful tidal river basin bounded by ancient ridges, vast salt marshes, and the narrowest strategic land bridge in eastern North America.

```
       [ North: Richibucto / Elsipogtog / Northumberland Coast ]
                                  ▲
                                  │ (Old Railbeds & Game Trails)
  [ West: Saint John Valley ] ─── [ THE BEND / PETKOTKWEYAK ] ─── [ East: Tantramar & Kweswe'g ]
                                  │ (Tidal Mudflats & Bore)
                                  ▼
                     [ South: Bay of Fundy / Amlamgog ]
```

## 2. Key Landmarks & Tactical Zones

### 1. Petkotkweyak ("The Bend" / Urban Core)
* **Colonial Name:** Moncton Downtown & Riverfront.
* **Status:** High-Risk Biohazard & Horde Trap (Post-Week 2).
* **Strategic Value:** High concentration of raw materials (hardware stores, timber yards, libraries), but hazardous due to rotting grocery warehouses and confined streets.
* **Natural Dynamic:** The sharp bow-like bend of the river cuts off direct southern foot travel unless crossing at the silt flats during precise low-tide windows.

---

### 2. The Mudflats & The Tidal Bore (The "Dead Marshes")
* **Location:** The river basin stretching from The Bend down through Hopewell and Shepody.
* **The Mud Quicksand:** Bottomless, sticky red clay silt. A human or zombie with heavy footing sinks past the knees in under three paces.
* **The 30-Foot Tide:** Twice daily, the Fundy tidal surge rushes up the river. Mindless infected trapped in the mud are swept away and drowned without firing a single shot.
* **Protagonist Usage:** The protagonist uses elevated boardwalks and knows the firm gravel gravel-bars. He actively lures infected clusters onto the mudflats at falling tide.

---

### 3. The Isthmus of Chignecto (*Kweswe'g* & The Tantramar Marshes)
* **Location:** The narrow 24 km land neck connecting Nova Scotia to mainland New Brunswick.
* **Tactical Significance:** The **Thermopylae of the North**. It is the only overland route into the Nova Scotian peninsula.
* **Terrain:** Vast, wind-swept salt marshes, elevated dikes, and old CN rail lines. Endless sightlines make surprise ambushes impossible. 
* **The Wall:** Whoever controls the elevated railbeds across the Tantramar controls all movement between the mainland and the eastern peninsula.

---

### 4. Major Urban Choke Points & Deathtraps

* **The Petitcodiac Causeway:**
  * The primary highway crossing between the north and south banks. Choked solid with hundreds of rusted, collided vehicles. A prime ambush spot for infected trapped in car rows.
* **Gunningsville Bridge:**
  * High-span bridge. Subject to severe crosswinds and funneling. Impassable on foot without clearing dozens of abandoned vehicles.
* **Wheeler Boulevard & Mountain Road:**
  * Commercial corridors lined with strip malls and supercenters. After Week 2, these are methane-filled rot zones surrounded by wandering hordes.

## 3. The Shadow Network: Trails & Portages (No Roads)

The protagonist avoids all paved roads, moving exclusively via unmapped natural corridors:

```
[ The Dobson Trail Corridor ]          ──▶ 58 km wilderness highway running south to the Fundy Highlands
[ Ancient Petitcodiac-Shediac Portage ] ──▶ Connects Fundy Basin to Northumberland Strait
[ The Abandoned Rail Corridors ]       ──▶ Elevated gravel berms through marshes (Clear Sight)
[ Sunken Stream Ravines ]               ──▶ Hidden below ground level (Zero Silhouette)
```

1. **The Dobson Trail Wilderness Corridor:**
   * A 58-kilometer continuous wilderness trail that begins right at the edge of the river basin and runs south through the rugged Albert County hills and hemlock valleys all the way to the Fundy Highlands. 
   * It is his primary overland artery: 100% off-pavement, covered by spruce and maple canopy, with natural freshwater spring crossings every few miles.
2. **The Ancient Fundy–Northumberland Portage:**
   * The historic trail connecting the tidal headwaters of the Petkotkweyak to the Shediac River basin. Allows travel from the southern bay to the northern strait completely under forest canopy.
3. **The Abandoned Intercolonial Railbeds:**
   * Gravel beds elevated 6–10 feet above the surrounding marsh. Free of mud, dry, and provides 360-degree line-of-sight while allowing an immediate dive into roadside alder thickets if movement is spotted.
4. **Humphrey's Brook & Hall's Creek Ravines:**
   * Deep, wooded stream cuts cutting straight through the outer suburbs. Walking the creek bed keeps a traveler's head below street grade, muffling noise and masking silhouettes.

## 4. Regional Settlements & Outposts

* **The Blind Siding & The Rock-Cut Depot (The Hidden Rail Stop):**
  * Tucked 300 yards northeast of the Stone Light inside a natural sandstone rock cut along the abandoned CN spur.
  * **Master Concealment (Counter-Scouting Design):** Designed specifically so that even observant, hostile scouts walking the tracks see nothing. The rail spur siding is hidden behind a living screen of dead spruce abatis and a counter-balanced brush-and-sandstone swing gate that mimics a natural landslide.
  * **No Footprints on the Ballast:** Kwitn steps only on the iron rails and moss cushions; the gravel entry is brushed out with a cedar broom after every draisine run.
  * **The False Washout:** 200 yards past the siding, the tracks cross a narrow ravine where center ties are removed over a 20-foot drop—discouraging travelers from exploring further down the spur.
  * **The Low-Profile Loading Bench:** Inside the rock alcove, a split-cedar cargo deck sits flush with the draisine bed for quiet, zero-strain unloading of iron, coal, and winter grain sacks.

* **The Protagonist’s Creek Camp (The Living Home):**
  * Tucked 4 km up a spring-fed tributary in a sunken hemlock hollow north of The Bend. 
  * Low-profile, half-timbered camp with a smokeless woodstove, fenced *Three Sisters* plots, and a freshwater gravity flume. Completely invisible from the river and ridgeline.
* **The Old Stone Light on the Bluff (The "Clacks" Tower):**
  * A decommissioned 19th-century cut-granite lighthouse perched on the rocky headland 400 yards above his camp.
  * **Role:** Observation post, weather station, emergency fallback citadel, and visual optical telegraph ("The Clacks").
  * **Tactical Advantage:** His actual dwelling remains hidden in the trees. The stone tower handles long-range signaling across Shepody Bay and the Tantramar marshes without exposing his sleeping quarters or pantry.
* **Amlamgog (Fort Folly / Memramcook Basin):**
  * Historic Mi'kmaw gathering area downstream. Excellent high-ground vantage points over Shepody Bay and access to deep-water fishing.
* **The High Ridges (Lutes Mountain / Magnetic Ridge):**
  * The northern high ground overlooking the entire river valley. Provides 15 km of visual scouting over weather patterns, smoke plumes, and horde migrations.
