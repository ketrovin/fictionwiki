* **Seasoned Stave Stash (Curing in the Shed):**
  * 3 split staves of white ash (air-seasoned for 2 years, ready for new bows).
  * 2 seasoned rock-maple billets (dense hardwood for clubs, crossguards, and adze handles).
  * 12 Port Orford cedar arrow blanks + bundle of wild turkey and goose wing feathers.

### The Fishing, Watercraft & Weir Equipment
* **The Hand-Built 16-Foot Cedar Canoe:** 
  * Built using steam-bent northern white cedar ribs, spruce root lashings (*wapnaqn*), and waterproofed with pine pitch and bear grease.
  * Weighs only 55 lbs (easily portaged by one man on shoulder yokes across trail divides), draws only 4 inches of water, and carries 800 lbs of gear/passengers silently down the Petkotkweyak.
* **Hand-Carved Ash Paddles (x2):** Ottertail-style narrow blades shaped with a crooked knife for silent, splash-free deep-water strokes.
* **Hand Gaff & Dip Nets:** For landing fish trapped in the shallow weir basins.
* **Split Cedar Weir Stakes (Pre-Cut):** 60 sharpened four-foot cedar stakes ready to expand the weir funnels in the tidal mud.
* **Heavy Hemp Cordage & Fish-Drying Racks:** 4 portable cedar racks for cold-smoking and sun-drying fish with rock salt.

## 4. Homesteading & Food Assets (In-Progress Build)

Because he had already started his off-grid transition, his pantry and camp are equipped for sustainable living, not short-term snacking:

* **Cooking & Water:**
  * 10-inch and 12-inch Lodge cast iron skillets + 5-quart cast iron Dutch oven with bail handle.
  * 4-gallon enameled stockpot for boiling large batches of water.
  * Hand-built gravity sand-and-charcoal water filter barrel beneath the roof drip line.
* **Food Stores on Hand (Day Zero):**
  * **Rock Salt:** 50-pound sack of coarse non-iodized salt (essential for dry-curing fish and meat).
  * **Pantry Staples:** 25 lbs of rolled oats, 20 lbs of dried navy beans, 10 lbs of lard, 5 lbs of loose black tea, and 2 gallons of local maple syrup.
  * **Foraged Caches:** 4 glass jars of dried chaga fungus (tea/fire tinder), dried fiddleheads from the spring harvest, and dried sweetgrass braids.
* **Fenced Harvestable Plots & Managed Forage Zones:**
  * **Wattle-Fenced Garden & Forage Patches:** Hand-split cedar post and woven alder brush fences protecting delicate crops from deer, raccoons, and clumsy wandering dead:
    * Fenced *Three Sisters* plot (flint corn, climbing beans, squash).
    * Protected native groundnut (*Apios americana*) and wild Jerusalem artichoke tubers.
    * Cultivated sweetgrass and medicinal sweet flag (*Wi'kpe'k*) patch along the moist creek bank.
* **The Tidal "Live-Pantry" Weirs:**
  * **3 Shallow Pit Weirs:** Woven brush funnels leading into excavated stone basins. When the 30-foot Fundy tide recedes, trapped flounder, tomcod, and striped bass remain swimming in 2 feet of cool, clean water. Fish stay alive for days if horde movement keeps him pinned down.
* **Active Trap Lines (Conditional Use):**
  * **Spring-Pole & Brass Wire Hare Snares:** Set along dense spruce runways on the high ridge. Checked strictly when the wind is favorable and bird chatter confirms the trail is clear.
  * **Heavy Log Deadfalls:** For porcupine, beaver, or raccoon near watercourses—passive protein without firing a shot.
* **Perimeter Fences & Anti-Zed Traps (And Their Realistic Limits):**
  * **Brush Abatis & Wattle Defenses:** Sharpened spruce slash-piles and woven hazel barriers designed to redirect movement rather than act as impenetrable fortresses.
  * **Gravity Trip-Logs & Pit Catches:** Concealed trip wires that trigger deadfall logs or drop zombies into mud pits.
  * **The Reality / Limitation:** Single zombies get tripped, impaled, or stuck in mud with 100% success. However, clustered groups can trample wattle fences, pile up into ditches until others walk over them, or snap trip lines through sheer dead weight. He treats traps as **early-warning alarms and delay tactics**, not magical forcefields.

## 6. What He Consciously DOES NOT Have (Realistic Limitations)

To keep his story grounded and avoid the "unlimited superhero" trope:
* **No Guns or Ammo:** He intentionally chose not to buy firearms, viewing them as loud liabilities that rust in salt fog and foster false confidence.
* **No Solar Generator / Electronics:** No fragile inverters, lithium batteries, or solar panels to troubleshoot or guard.
* **No Running Water:** Relies entirely on a natural gravity spring 80 yards up the ridge and clean rainwater collection.
* **Incomplete Infrastructure:** His root cellar is only half-dug, and his winter firewood stack (currently 3 cords) needs another 4–5 cords split before October.
